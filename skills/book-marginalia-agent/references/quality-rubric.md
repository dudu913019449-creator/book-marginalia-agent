# Marginalia quality rubric

Use this rubric to revise weak comments or compare variants. A worthwhile note should pass most of these checks.

## Faithfulness

- Can the author's claim be recognized from the explanation?
- Does the note distinguish the author's view from the agent's view?
- Is criticism based on the passage or its surrounding context rather than a stock disagreement?

## Specificity

- Does the comment point to a meaningful phrase, assumption, incentive, contrast, or consequence?
- Would the comment still make sense if attached to a completely different passage? If yes, it is too generic.
- Remove openings such as “这句话深刻地揭示了” unless the following text adds concrete information.

## Personal relevance

- Is the connection based on a verified reader fact or explicitly labelled inference?
- Is the memory source visible?
- Would a neutral question be more honest than a forced personal connection?

## Critical value

- Does the challenge identify a boundary, missing premise, structural cause, counterexample, or evidence gap?
- Keep “wrong”, “incomplete”, and “not verifiable” distinct.
- Do not treat disagreement as intelligence. Sometimes the strongest note is an accurate explanation.

## Usefulness

- Does the final question or action change what the reader might notice, test, decide, or do?
- Is it small enough to act on?
- Does it avoid turning every literary or philosophical passage into a productivity task?

## Compression

- Could any sentence be removed without losing meaning?
- Default to four short parts and one optional standalone line.
- Preserve nuance before adding length.

## Save threshold

A note is worth saving when at least one is true:

- it clarifies a difficult idea;
- it captures a reader-specific connection with a real source;
- it exposes a non-obvious limitation or counterargument;
- it produces a question or action the reader wants to revisit;
- the reader edits or explicitly endorses it.
